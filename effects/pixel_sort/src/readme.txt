Pixel Sort {v}: runs of pixels sorted into streaks, live, along any angle. touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/pixel_sort.md

Pixel sorting rearranges the pixels of a picture in order, darkest to brightest for example, along its rows or columns. Sorting whole rows smears the picture into gradients, so the art form sorts only runs of pixels, spans: say the pixels whose brightness lies between two thresholds. Those melt into streaks while the rest of the picture stays as it was, and a face or a sky seems to pour or drip.

This effect really sorts: every span of every line, every frame, in full. The lines can run at any angle. Spans can be the pixels between two brightness thresholds, the stretches between the picture's edges, what lies inside a depth map or mask on input 3, or whole lines, all optionally cut at random into many lengths. A span sorts by brightness, hue or saturation. The music turns the sort end over end on the bars, tears thin streaks on the beats, widens the spans as it gets louder, and sorts every line whole on a drop.

USE
Drag pixel_sort.tox into your network. It shows TouchDesigner's sample movie, a tree against bright foliage, with the light between the branches pouring down. Wire any TOP into input 1, and the result is on output 1 at the input's size. You can also wire audio or beat channels into input 2 and a depth map or mask into input 3 (below). Open Window shows the picture full size, and Esc closes the window.

THE PAGE
Spans picks which runs sort: Brightness (between Low and High), Edges (between edges stronger than Edge), Depth or Mask (where input 3 lies between Low and High) or Whole Lines. Cut Every cuts spans at random points about that many pixels apart. Sort By sorts by Brightness, Hue or Saturation. Angle is the way each span's values rise, 270 (the default) putting the brightest at the bottom, and Spin turns it, at most {spin:g} degrees a second. Flips, Beat Kick, Loudness, Song Moments and Length are the music's (below).

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input. Run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. With Flips at On Bars, the default, the sort turns end over end on the one of each bar, so every span's bright end swaps with its dark end. On Beats flips it on every beat, Steady every {steady:g} s, and Off never. On every beat three thin slivers of lines sort whole from end to end, against the grain, for {tear:g} s (Beat Kick says how wide). Louder music widens the spans, so more of the picture sorts (Loudness), moving Low toward black and High toward white. With Song Moments on, a build widens them as it rises, and a drop sorts every line whole for Length seconds (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo, and a bar every 4 beats. That clock is experimental and can still add or miss beats. TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. With nothing wired, the sort flips every {steady:g} s and tears with each flip.

DEPTH OR MASK (input 3, optional)
With Spans at Depth or Mask, input 3 says what sorts: grayscale, brighter nearer (or inside a mask), between Low and High. Low 0.5 and High 1 sort what lies inside a mask, or what is near in a depth map, and leave the rest as it was. With nothing on input 3, input 1's brightness stands in.

FLASHING
A flip changes every span at once, and so does a drop's whole sort, so both count as flashes. They pass one gate that follows the Flashing parameter. Off never lets one through, Calm (the default) at most one a second, and Up to 3 per second is the WCAG 2.3.1 limit, which no setting exceeds. After the gate a budget keeps this effect well under that limit. The beat's tears cover too little of the picture to count, and Flashing Off stops them too. Span ends can flicker with the picture's own noise, so a pixel joins or leaves a span only once it is clearly inside or outside the range (hysteresis), and loudness widens the spans gradually. Turning the angle moves the steps in the lines, and every span a step crosses sorts anew, so Spin stops at {spin:g} degrees a second: faster, whole spans changed often enough to flash with Flashing Off.

INSIDE
key works out, for each pixel, the value it sorts by, whether it lies in a span and whether its line is cut there, reading its own last frame (key_fb) for the hysteresis and input 3 through depth. sort is a compute shader with one workgroup a line. It takes the line's pixels toward Angle, finds where each span starts, sorts the whole line once by span start, value and place, which sorts each span within itself and leaves the spans where they were, and writes each pixel back where it lands. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in), and counts and steps turn its counts into steps. beat_on, bar_on, steady_on and drop_on pick the flips and drops asked for, and flash_limit, flash and flash_budget gate them. flip_n and flip_parity turn the sort over, and whole sorts every line whole on a drop. beat_env and beat_n open each beat's tears, level_smooth and widen widen the spans, and turned turns Angle by Spin.

CREDITS
- Pixel sorting with threshold spans as an art form, after Kim Asendorf's ASDFPixelSort (Processing, 2010, https://github.com/kimasendorf/ASDFPixelSort, no license stated). Followed, not copied.
- The span kinds (between two brightness thresholds, between edges, whole lines with random lengths) and the defaults Low 0.25 and High 0.8, after satyarth's pixelsort (Python, MIT, https://github.com/satyarth/pixelsort). Followed, not copied.
- The bitonic sorting network, K. E. Batcher, "Sorting networks and their applications" (AFIPS Spring Joint Computer Conference, 1968). Sorting each span inside one sort of the whole line, by a key that starts with where the span starts, is a widely known technique (a segmented sort). Where each span starts comes from a parallel max-scan, after W. D. Hillis and G. L. Steele, "Data parallel algorithms" (Communications of the ACM, 1986).
- Edges by the Sobel operator, and hue and saturation by the usual HSV formulas: widely known techniques.
- Chris Wellons' lowbias32 integer hash (hash-prospector, public domain) places the cuts and the tears.
- Read during the survey, not used: MaxMainio's Sort (MIT, whole rows or columns), aaronmylespereira's pixelSorting (a feedback look from a tutorial), Lou Evoy's Pixel Sort TOP (paid, NVIDIA only), a 2017 forum port of a Shadertoy sorter, and elekktronaut's Pixel Sorting tutorial.
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input). A kick is a jump in the low band's loudness, and the beat clock takes its tempo from the gaps between kicks, each voting for the beat lengths it is a whole multiple of. That is inter-onset-interval tempo induction, the idea behind S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
- Flash limits from WCAG 2.3.1 (W3C, Three Flashes or Below Threshold), measured with this library's tools.
Everything else (the digital lines at any angle, the compute shader, the hysteresis, the tears and the music) is written for this library. MIT.
