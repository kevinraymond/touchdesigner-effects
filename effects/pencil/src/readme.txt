Pencil {v}: the live picture as a pencil drawing, sketchy lines along its edges and its tones in pencil strokes.
touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/pencil.md

A pencil drawing is two things at once. Lines give the shapes, quick strokes that run past the corners and cross where two edges meet. Tone gives the light and shade, strokes laid over each other until an area is as dark as it should be, with most of the paper left white.

This effect draws the live picture both ways, after Lu, Xu and Jia's pencil drawing paper. It finds the picture's edges and draws them with short straight strokes in eight directions, so the lines look drawn rather than traced. It matches the picture's tones to those of real pencil drawings, mostly paper with a band of mild strokes and a few dark accents, and draws them with pencil strokes. Sketch lays short, light strokes every way, as soft graphite shading, as the paper does. Hatching lays long parallel strokes that follow the edges and cross only in the darks, as an illustrator hatches. On the beats of the music the drawing is redrawn, as the next drawing of a hand-drawn animation would be.

USE
Drag pencil.tox into your network. It shows TouchDesigner's sample movie of a river running over rocks, drawn in pencil. Wire any TOP into input 1, and the result is on output 1 at the input's size. You can also wire audio or beat channels into input 2 and a depth map or mask into input 3 (below). Open Window shows the picture full size, and Esc closes the window.

THE PAGE
Style picks Sketch, Hatching or Lines (the lines alone). Colors draws in Graphite, Colored Pencil (the picture's own colors, the drawing as their brightness), Blue Pencil (an animator's blue) or Light on Dark (white pencil on black paper, drawing where the picture is light). Lines says how dark the lines are, and Line Length how far they run past their edges. Shading says how dark the tones are, from mostly paper to darker with more dark accents; the tones follow the picture over a second, so a light walking in doesn't swing the whole drawing at once. Stroke Size is the pencil strokes' size. Follow Movement says how much the strokes travel with what moves in the picture rather than staying on the paper while the picture slides under them. Texture is how much the paper's grain shows in the graphite. Depth Fade, Depth Type and Max Distance are for input 3, and Beat Kick, Loudness and Song Moments are the music's.

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input. Run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. Each beat redraws the drawing: the lines turn a little and the pencil strokes move (Beat Kick says how much). Louder music presses the pencil harder and quiet passages lighter (Loudness), eased over half a second. With Song Moments on, a build draws longer, looser lines as it rises, and a drop draws them twice as long for Length seconds (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo. That clock is experimental and can still add or miss beats. TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. With nothing wired, the drawing holds still.

DEPTH OR MASK (input 3, optional)
With a depth map on input 3, Depth Fade draws far things lighter, as an artist leaves the distance faint, up to leaving the farthest as paper. With a mask (white inside), what lies outside it fades, so the drawing keeps to the subject. Depth Type reads depth cameras too. The RealSense and Kinect TOPs give distance from 0 to 1 (black where the camera got no reading, which is left out), and the Kinect Azure TOP gives meters, scaled by Max Distance. With nothing on input 3 nothing fades.

FLASHING
Pencil doesn't flash: it adds no light of its own, and a beat's redraw only moves fine strokes, which keeps every area's tone. So it has no Flashing setting. Two things keep the drawing from flickering where the picture barely does. The lines multiply small changes in the picture's edges, so the edges are followed over a fifth of a second, and a video's noise doesn't flicker them. The tones are matched over the whole picture and capped so that no narrow band of the picture's tones is stretched over every tone. A drawing has few dark areas, though, so a picture's darks come out mid-gray, and changes too dark to count as flashes in the picture can count in the drawing. Measured under a beat far busier than music, no setting added more than half a flash a second to the test clips' own, and nothing came near WCAG's three (docs page, Accessibility).

PERFORMANCE
The lines cost most of the frame: each pixel reads eight line segments twice, whatever Line Length is. Follow Movement measures the picture's motion (optical flow), which costs about a quarter of the frame; at 0 it doesn't run. The pencil strokes are drawn once, when the effect loads.

INSIDE
edges is the picture's gradient, eased over a fifth of a second (edges_fb is its last frame). classify gives each pixel the direction whose line segment gathers the most gradient around it, and shape spreads each direction's pixels back along its segment: the lines. histogram counts the picture's levels, each capped, and tone turns them into a table of 256 levels, each of the picture's levels as the tone a pencil drawing gives it, eased over a second (tone_fb is its last frame). strokes draws the pencil strokes once: six levels of hatching, from a few parallel strokes to cross-hatching, and Sketch's tonal pattern of short strokes laid every way (strokes_dark holds hatching's levels 4 to 6). tensor, tensor_h, tensor_v, tensor_ease and flow (lib structure_tensor) find the direction of the picture's edges for Hatching, and orient passes it on only then (small is the picture at half size for it). lum8, lum4, change, clock, lk8 and lk4 (lib optical_flow) measure the picture's motion, pace and motion (lib flow_speed) turn it into a speed, drift carries the strokes' two offsets along with it (drift_fb is its last frame), and drifted passes them on only while Follow Movement is above 0. draw multiplies the lines by the tone drawn in pencil strokes, on paper, in the colors chosen. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in): counts and steps turn its counts into steps, beat_n counts the beats that redraw, drop_env times a drop's long lines and level_smooth eases the loudness. Input 3 goes through depth and near (lib depth_near).

CREDITS
- The lines, the tone model and the pencil texture: C. Lu, L. Xu and J. Jia, "Combining Sketch and Tone for Pencil Drawing Production" (NPAR 2012), written from the paper. Two changes: the texture's density comes from a closed form, beta = ln J / ln H with H blurred, where the paper solves a smoothed least squares, and the eight directions span half a turn.
- Hatching: tonal art maps after E. Praun, H. Hoppe, M. Webb and A. Finkelstein, "Real-Time Hatching" (SIGGRAPH 2001), here turned along the picture's edges rather than a surface's curvature.
- The tones capped as contrast-limited histogram equalization caps its histogram: S. M. Pizer et al., "Adaptive Histogram Equalization and Its Variations" (Computer Vision, Graphics, and Image Processing 39, 1987) and K. Zuiderveld, "Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization" (Graphics Gems IV, 1994), here over the whole picture.
- Strokes that travel with the picture: advected textures, two offsets carried by the motion and faded between, after N. Max and B. Becker, "Flow Visualization Using Moving Textures" (ICASE/LaRC Symposium on Visualizing Time-Varying Data, 1995) and F. Neyret, "Advected Textures" (SCA 2003). The motion is this library's Lucas-Kanade optical flow (B. D. Lucas and T. Kanade, "An Iterative Image Registration Technique with an Application to Stereo Vision", 1981), shared with Datamosh, Fluid and VHS + CRT.
- The direction of the picture's edges: the smoothed structure tensor (lib structure_tensor, shared with Paint and Reaction-Diffusion), after J. E. Kyprianidis, H. Kang and J. Doellner, "Image and Video Abstraction by Anisotropic Kuwahara Filtering" (Pacific Graphics 2009) and J. E. Kyprianidis and J. Doellner (NPAR 2011), with the derivative filter of B. Jaehne, H. Scharr and S. Koerkel (1999).
- The paper's grain: gradient noise after K. Perlin, "Improving Noise" (SIGGRAPH 2002), shared with Paint (lib paper.glsl). Strokes fall where Chris Wellons' lowbias32 integer hash says (hash-prospector, public domain). erf by M. Abramowitz and I. Stegun's Handbook of Mathematical Functions, 7.1.26.
- Depth Type: the RealSense, Kinect and Kinect Azure TOPs' depth, as their help pages describe it; the depth reading (lib depth_near) is shared with Fluid, Paint, Particles and Point Cloud.
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input): a kick is a jump in the low band's loudness; the beat clock takes its tempo from the gaps between kicks, each voting for the beat lengths it is a whole multiple of: inter-onset-interval tempo induction, the idea behind S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
Everything else is written for this library: the pencil strokes drawn by a shader, the eight directions read whole and mixed, the easing of the edges and the tones, the redraw on the beat and the depth fade. MIT.
