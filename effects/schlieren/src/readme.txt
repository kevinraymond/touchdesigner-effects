Schlieren {v}: the warm air rising off the live picture's subject made visible, as a schlieren camera shows it.
touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/schlieren.md

Warm air is invisible, but it bends light a little, since air gets thinner as it warms and light slows less in it. A schlieren camera makes that bending visible. It lights the scene with a beam focused through a point and puts a blade at that point, so light the air bent one way is cut off and light bent the other way gets through. The air then shows as gray relief, as if carved and lit from the side: the heat rising off a hand, a person's warm plume, a candle's flame, a cough. A colored filter in place of the blade shows the bending as color, no blade at all shows its sharpest folds as bright and dark lines (a shadowgraph), and light that went round the air, brought back to meet the light that went through it, shows it as fringes (an interferogram).

This effect gives the live picture that air. What stands in front in the depth map (or is inside a mask), the picture's lights and whatever moves warm the air above them, and the warm air rises in a plume that sways and curls, stirred by whatever moves through it. The picture is seen through the air, shifted a little where it bends, and the subject and the lights stay opaque, so the air shows around them. The air is made up from the picture, never measured.

USE
Drag schlieren.tox into your network. It shows TouchDesigner's sample movie of grass in front of a building with a soft white light circling over it, and warm air rises off the light and trails behind it within a second. Wire any TOP into input 1, and the result is on output 1 at the input's size. You can also wire audio or beat channels into input 2 and a depth map or mask into input 3 (below). Open Window shows the picture full size, and Esc closes the window. Reset clears the air: no heat and still.

THE PAGE
Style picks Knife Edge, Rainbow, Shadowgraph or Interferogram (below). Strength says how strongly the bending shows. Edge Angle turns the blade: at 0 it stands upright and shows the plume's sides, at 90 it lies flat and shows its tops, and in Rainbow it turns the colors. Fringes is how many straight fringes an Interferogram lays across a frame height, which the warm air bends; at 0 only the air's own fringes show. Backdrop says how much of the picture shows behind the air: at 0 the picture is a schlieren photograph, an even gray field with the subject as a dark silhouette.

Heat says how quickly warm things heat the air above them, Rise how hard the warm air rises, Swirl how much it curls into eddies, and Linger how long its heat lasts once carried away. Stir says how much the picture's movement drags the air along.

Warm by Movement, Warm by Nearness and Warm by Light say what warms the air (below), and Input 3 Holds, Sensitivity, Depth Type and Max Distance read input 3. Beat Puffs, Loudness, Band Heights, Bar Turn and Song Moments are the music's.

STYLES
Knife Edge shows the air as gray relief over the picture, lit from the side the blade faces: one side of a plume light, the other dark. Where the air bends the light hard the relief saturates, as a real blade's cutoff does.

Rainbow puts a color filter in place of the blade, as Rheinberg's color schlieren does: the way the light bends picks the color, from the spectrum, so a plume shows its sides in different colors, and how hard it bends says how much color shows.

Shadowgraph has no blade at all. The light gathers where the bending converges and leaves where it spreads, so the air's sharpest folds show as bright and dark lines, like light on the bottom of a pool.

Interferogram lays straight fringes across the picture, as an interferometer whose two beams meet at a slight tilt does, and the warm air, slowing the light that crosses it, bends them around the plume.

WHAT WARMS THE AIR
With a depth map or mask on input 3, what stands in front of its surroundings, or is inside the mask, warms the air above it (Warm by Nearness) and hides the air behind it. Whatever moves warms it too, quickly, and leaves it to cool behind it (Warm by Movement), and so do the picture's lights, as a lamp or a candle heats the air above it (Warm by Light): what is bright and brighter than what is around it, so a bright sky isn't a light. A light hides the air behind it too. A faint movement (haze drifting through a dark room) doesn't count. The air rises in a column above whatever is warm and carries the heat up with it, at most 0.4 frame heights a second, and the heat cools as it mixes into the room.

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input. Run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. Each beat puffs the warm air up at once, so it rolls up into eddies, as often as Flashing allows (Beat Puffs). A puff is full strength while a small share of the picture is warm (a person, a hand) and weaker where more of it is, so it never heaves the whole frame's air at once. Louder music heats the air faster and quiet passages slower (Loudness). The music's seven bands each heat one height of the frame, the lowest at the bottom and the highest at the top, so the bass warms the feet and the cymbals the head (Band Heights). Each bar turns the blade a little, slowly and at most once every 2 seconds (Bar Turn, Knife Edge and Rainbow). With Song Moments on, a drop sends up a big puff (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo. That clock is experimental and can still add or miss beats. TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. With nothing wired, the air rises at its own pace.

DEPTH OR MASK (input 3, optional)
A depth map, brighter nearer, says what stands in front of its surroundings: that warms the air and hides the air behind it. Floors and walls don't count as standing out, someone cut off by the left or right edge of the frame drops out, and feet merge with the floor they stand on. With Input 3 Holds on Mask, what is inside the mask (white inside: MediaPipe, the NVIDIA Background TOP) does instead. Sensitivity says how easily something counts as standing out, or how gray a mask counts as inside. Depth Type reads depth cameras too: the RealSense and Kinect TOPs give distance from 0 to 1 (black where the camera got no reading, which is left out), and the Kinect Azure TOP gives meters, scaled by Max Distance. With nothing on input 3, movement and light warm the air, and only the lights are opaque.

FLASHING
A beat's puff throws the warm air up over a wide area at once, so it goes through the Flashing setting: Off never puffs, Calm (the default) at most once a second, Up to 3 per second at most three times. A puff is weaker where much of the picture is warm, the air never rises faster than 0.4 frame heights a second, the blade turns at most once every 2 seconds, and every pixel goes through a red guard. The flashes were measured on the output under a beat far busier than music, every style and five clips, each within its setting's rule. Strength, Rise and Beat Puffs all at 1 together on footage that moves all over measured half a flash a second over WCAG's 3, so keep one of them lower there (docs page, Accessibility).

PERFORMANCE
Schlieren costs about 3 ms a frame on the GPU and 0.75 ms on the CPU at 1280 x 720 on an M2 Pro. The picture's optical flow, the air's pressure solve and the look cost the most, about 0.6 ms each.

INSIDE
Unwired, ball (a soft white light on a figure of eight) over sample (demo) stands in for input 1. Input 3 goes through depth, near (nearness at a quarter size) and find (what stands out, or the mask), lib depth_near and depth_find. subject says, at a quarter of the picture's size, what warms the air (what stands out, the lights) and what is opaque. lib optical_flow (lum8, lum8p, seen, change, change_avg, clock_fb, clock, lk8) measures what moves, and lib flow_speed (pace, motion) turns it into a speed held between the input's frames. warmth (lib warmth, with warmth_fb) blends what warms and what moves into one field that warms quickly and cools slowly. lift says where the air rises: in a column above what is warm, wavered by slow noise. air steps the air's velocity (velocity_fb reads it back): carried by itself, kept swirling, lifted, puffed on the beats and dragged by the picture's movement; lib incompressible (divergence, pressure with pressure_fb, velocity) keeps it from piling up. warm_area says how much of the picture is warm, which weakens a puff where much of it is. heat (with heat_fb) carries the heat on the air at half the picture's size and heats it where warm. bend works out how the heat bends the light, and look shows the picture through it in the Style's look. clear is the nothing every Feedback TOP starts from, and Reset sends them back to it. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in): counts and steps turn its counts into steps, ask, flash_limit and flash let beats and drops through as often as Flashing allows, puff_env and drop_env shape a puff, bar_gap, bars and turn_lag count the bars (at most one every 2 seconds) and ease the blade's turn, level_smooth eases the loudness and bands_smooth the bands.

Settles, "Schlieren and Shadowgraph Techniques: Visualizing Phenomena in Transparent Media" (Springer, 2001): how warm air bends light, the knife edge's relief and its cutoff, color schlieren with a Rheinberg filter, the shadowgraph as the bending's convergence.
- The four looks rendered from a simulated field: C. Brownlee, V. Pegoraro, S. Shankar, P. McCormick and C. Hansen, "Physically-Based Interactive Flow Visualization Based on Schlieren and Interferometry Experimental Techniques" (IEEE TVCG 17(11), 2011), whose shadowgraph, schlieren, color filter and interferometry views this follows, in two dimensions. Followed, not copied.
- The air: J. Stam, "Stable Fluids" (SIGGRAPH 1999), as M. Harris laid it out for the GPU ("Fast Fluid Dynamics Simulation on the GPU", GPU Gems ch. 38, 2004), its pressure solve shared with Fluid (lib incompressible); vorticity confinement from R. Fedkiw, J. Stam and H. W. Jensen, "Visual Simulation of Smoke" (SIGGRAPH 2001).
- The heat carried by MacCormack's step as A. Selle, R. Fedkiw, B. Kim, Y. Liu and J. Rossignac made it unconditionally stable, "An Unconditionally Stable MacCormack Method" (J. Sci. Comput. 35, 2008), with their clamp.
- Rainbow's colors: the CIE 1931 color matching functions as C. Wyman, P.-P. Sloan and P. Shirley fit them, "Simple Analytic Approximations to the CIE XYZ Color Matching Functions" (JCGT 2(2), 2013), shared with Lens Flare (lib spectrum.glsl); the red guard (lib red_guard.glsl) keeps its reds short of WCAG's red flash line.
- What warms the air: the library's warmth (lib warmth, shared with Frost), its optical flow (lib optical_flow and flow_speed, shared with Datamosh, Fluid and others) and its depth reading (lib depth_near and depth_find). The noise that wavers the lift uses Chris Wellons' lowbias32 integer hash (hash-prospector, public domain); the picture's bending is resampled Catmull-Rom (lib common.glsl).
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input): a kick is a jump in the low band's loudness; the beat clock takes its tempo from the gaps between kicks, inter-onset-interval tempo induction, the idea behind S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
- Flash limits: WCAG 2.3.1 (W3C, Three Flashes or Below Threshold), measured with this library's tools.
- Not used: measuring real air from a camera's view of a textured background (background-oriented schlieren), MIT's US 10,636,149 and US 9,710,917; nothing here is measured, the air is made up from the picture. A displayed dye carried on a fluid, ByteDance's US 12,694,597, and a fluid moved by a tracked object's change of speed, ByteDance's US 12,182,906: the heat here is never shown as a color, only by how it bends the light, it is carried by MacCormack's corrected step, and nothing is detected or tracked; movement drags the air only as the whole picture's optical flow. A velocity worked out from the very density it carries, Huawei's pending US 2025/0117558: the air rises above the picture's warmth, which is made afresh every frame, and the heat it carries never pushes it.
Everything else is written for this library: the picture as the source of the heat, the rising column above what is warm, the subject and the lights kept opaque, the gray schlieren photograph as a backdrop, the beat's puffs and the bands by height. MIT.
