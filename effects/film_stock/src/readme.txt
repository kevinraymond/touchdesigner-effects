Film Stock {v}: the picture shot on film and printed, its grain, weave, halation, flicker and dirt modeled from the film.
touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/film_stock.md

Film records light as grains: specks of silver (dye clouds, in color film) that develop where light fell, more of them where more light fell, so a photograph is made of specks, and the specks are the grain. A smaller film shows bigger grain on screen, since a Super 8 frame is a quarter the height of a 35 mm one and is blown up four times as far. The film is never quite still in the camera's or the projector's gate (weave), the lamp and shutter don't give quite the same light every frame (flicker), bright lights glow red where light bounces off the back of the film into its lowest layer (halation), the print picks up dust and scratches, and the frames step along at 24 a second.

This effect models those from how the film works rather than laying a noise texture over the picture. Each layer of the film answers the light by its characteristic curve, and its density is made of grains: for every pixel the number of developed grains is drawn from its Poisson law, with the grain's size set from the stock's published granularity, and once the grains are big enough to see, each one is drawn in its place. Then the negative is printed (or the reversal film shown), with the dyes' impurities and the stock's character. Gauge sets how much film a pixel covers, so 16 mm and Super 8 are grainier, softer, weave further and halate wider on screen, as they do. Day for Night turns daylight footage into a night scene the way a cinematographer's blue filter and underexposure did, with the eye's loss of detail at night. On the music, each beat kicks the film in the gate, louder passages push the grain, a drop splices the reel and a section change shows a cue mark.

USE
Drag film_stock.tox into your network: it shows TouchDesigner's sample movie (mist over a lake) on 16 mm color negative straight away. Wire any TOP into input 1; the result is on output 1, at the input's size. Optionally wire audio or beat channels into input 2 (below). Open Window shows it full size; Esc closes the window.

THE PAGE
Stock picks the film: Color Negative 500T (a modern tungsten negative, fine grain, warm skin), Color Negative 50D (the finest, sharpest daylight negative), Vintage Color Negative (a 1970s negative: coarser grain, softer, warmer, more halation, tamer colors), Color Reversal (a slide film, contrasty and saturated), Black and White 400 (a classic fast panchromatic negative, its grain from the published figure) and Black and White 100 (a modern tabular-grain negative, very fine). Gauge is the film's size: 35 mm, 16 mm or Super 8. Exposure is stops over or under, Contrast the print's contrast and Saturation the colors'. Grain scales the stock's grain (1 is the stock as measured, 0 none). Halation is how much light bounces off the base into the red layer around bright things. Weave is how far the film wanders in the gate, Flicker how much the light breathes, Dirt how much dust, hair and scratches the print carries. Frame Rate holds each frame as film does (24 a second, Super 8's 18, silent 16 mm's 16) or follows the source. Day for Night turns the scene into a night scene by that amount. Beat Kick is how far a beat kicks the film in the gate, Loudness how much louder music pushes the grain, and Song Moments lets Fosfora's drops splice the reel and its section changes show a cue mark. Flashing limits the kicks and splices.

THE FILM
Each layer of the emulsion (one in black and white, three in color: red, green and blue light each expose their own) turns its exposure into density by an S curve in log exposure, the characteristic curve: fog at the bottom, a straight part at the stock's gamma (about 0.6 for a negative), a shoulder at the top. A reversal stock's curve runs the other way and steeper. Density is grains. Developed grains are opaque disks scattered at random (the Boolean model of stochastic geometry, as Newson, Delon and Galerne used it for film grain), thick enough that a point is covered with the probability the density says. For each pixel the number of grains it holds is drawn from the Poisson law, and that number's wobble is the grain; when the grains are bigger than a third of a pixel each is drawn where it falls, as the eye then sees them one by one. The grains' size comes from the stock's published rms granularity by Nutting's formula, and from the Gauge: how many micrometers of film a pixel holds. The exposure is softened first by the stock's resolving power, and the light over white bounces off the base and exposes the red layer most, the green a little (halation). The negative is read a little soft, as a scanner reads it, and printed on a print stock with a steep curve, so the system's contrast comes out as a cinema print's; or the reversal is shown as it is. The dyes absorb a little of each other's colors, the stock tints its shadows and highlights its own way, the gate weaves and the lamp flickers, dirt lands on the frames, and every pixel goes through the red guard.

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input: run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. Each beat kicks the film in the gate (Beat Kick), louder passages push the grain coarser (Loudness), and with Song Moments on, a drop splices the reel (the frame line rolls through and the picture settles) and a section change shows a cue mark (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo (experimental: it can still add or miss beats) and its loudness from the sound; TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. Nothing wired: the film runs on its own, weaving, flickering and gathering dirt, with no kicks.

FLASHING
A beat's kick and a splice move the whole picture at once, so both go through one gate that follows the Flashing setting (Off: none; Calm, the default: at most one a second; Up to 3 per second, the WCAG 2.3.1 limit) and then a budget that keeps this effect to about 2 a second even at Up to 3. A kick is one jump and one settle, and a splice one roll and one settle. The flicker never swings the light more than 5%, under the 10% WCAG counts as a change, so it is never a flash at any setting. Grain changes every frame, as it does on film, but it is fine and evens out over any area WCAG counts; dirt is specks and hairs, far under WCAG's area. Halation makes reds, so every pixel goes through the red guard, which keeps reds just short of WCAG's saturated-red line.

PERFORMANCE
About {perf} ms on the GPU a frame at 1280 x 720 on an M2 Pro at the defaults (docs page, Performance). Big grain (Super 8, Grain over 2) costs more, since each grain is drawn.

INSIDE
hold keeps each frame for the film's frame rate (read back through hold_fb; cadence picks it or the source). gate moves the picture by the weave, the beat's kick and a splice's roll, in linear light with Exposure. night_src, night_fine, night_wide and night are Day for Night (scene picks it or the gate's picture). glare takes the light over white at an eighth of the size and halo spreads it as far as the base is thick. negative is the film: each layer's density and its grain, as transmittance. print reads it a little soft and prints it (or shows the reversal), with the dyes' impurities and the stock's character, Contrast and Saturation. show adds the flicker, the dirt and scratches, the cue mark, the red guard and sRGB. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in); counts and steps notice beats, drops and section changes; beat_on and drop_on ask for kicks and splices, flash_limit and flash gate them, flash_budget spaces them, kick_env shapes a kick, drop_env and drop_kind mark a splice's and splice_env rolls it; cue shows the cue mark; level_pick and level_smooth are the loudness; film_frame counts the film's frames and clock keeps the time.

CREDITS
- The grain: the Boolean model of stochastic geometry (G. Matheron, "Elements pour une theorie des milieux poreux", 1967; J. Serra, "Image Analysis and Mathematical Morphology", 1982) as A. Newson, J. Delon and B. Galerne used it for film grain, "A Stochastic Film Grain Model for Resolution-Independent Rendering" (Computer Graphics Forum 36(8), 2017) and, with N. Faraj, "Realistic Film Grain Rendering" (Image Processing On Line 7, 2017): grains as disks whose centers fall as a Poisson process with a density set by the pixel's gray level, with log-normal radii. Followed, not copied: the simulation here draws the count of grains in each pixel, or each grain, rather than their Monte Carlo sample of points.
- The grain's size from granularity: P. G. Nutting, "On the absorption of light in heterogeneous media" (Philosophical Magazine 26, 1913), the random-dot model's formula sigma_D^2 = 0.434 D a / A, with the diffuse rms granularity Kodak publishes for Tri-X 400 (17), T-Max 100 (8) and T-Max 400 (10), read at density 1.0 through a 48-micrometer aperture (Kodak data sheets F-4017 and F-4016), and Vision3 500T's granularity curves (H-1-5219), about 11 for red and green and 20 for blue. Resolving powers from the same sheets (Tri-X 100 lines/mm, T-Max 100 200 lines/mm) and the 500T's MTF curve. The other stocks are set by eye relative to those, and the stocks' colors and curves are this library's, shaped by eye after the look of each kind of film.
- The curves: F. Hurter and V. C. Driffield, "Photochemical Investigations and a New Method of Determination of the Sensitiveness of Photographic Plates" (Journal of the Society of Chemical Industry, 1890), the characteristic curve of density against log exposure; the negative-print system's contrast as cinema practice has it (a negative's gamma about 0.6, a print's about 2.8).
- Day for Night: W. B. Thompson, P. Shirley and J. A. Ferwerda, "A Spatial Post-Processing Algorithm for Images of Night Scenes" (Journal of Graphics Tools 7(1), 2002): the scotopic image blurred, its remaining edges sharpened by raising the band between two blurs to the power 1/1.25, the cones' colors kept where it is bright; the scotopic luminance by G. W. Larson, H. Rushmeier and C. Piatko, "A Visibility Matching Tone Reproduction Operator for High Dynamic Range Scenes" (IEEE TVCG 3(4), 1997). The wider blur here is a triangle, not the paper's second Gaussian.
- Halation, gate weave, flicker, cadence, dust and scratches: the physics as the film literature describes them (the base's reflection into the red layer, the sprockets' play, the shutter), written for this library; the cue mark is the projectionist's circle of the reel-change system.
- Film's frame heights: SMPTE 59 (35 mm camera aperture 22.05 x 16.03 mm), SMPTE 7 (16 mm, 10.26 x 7.49 mm) and SMPTE 154 (Super 8, 5.79 x 4.01 mm).
- Dice: Chris Wellons' lowbias32 integer hash (hash-prospector, public domain); Catmull-Rom resampling and the red guard from this library's lib/glsl.
- Not used, for three active US patents (docs page, Credits and license): the method of K. Zhang, J. Wang, D. Tian and T. N. Pappas, "Film Grain Rendering and Parameter Estimation" (ACM Transactions on Graphics 42(4), 2023), which derives the Boolean model's variance and correlation and synthesizes the grain as noise with those statistics (Northwestern's US 12,705,810), so the grain here is the model simulated, not noise shaped to its statistics; scaling unit noise by a function of the picture's brightness (Netflix's US 10,839,489, AV1's film grain), so no noise is scaled anywhere; and measuring grain from a reference image's tiles (Autodesk's US 7,873,210), so the stocks come from published figures.
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input): inter-onset-interval tempo induction after S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
- Flash limits: WCAG 2.3.1 (W3C, Three Flashes or Below Threshold), measured with this library's tools.
Everything else (the shaders, the stocks' tables, the simulation of the grain, the gate, the print) is written for this library. MIT.
