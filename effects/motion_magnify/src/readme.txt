Motion Magnify {v}: the live picture's movements made larger, a sway, a nod or a bounce drawn several times as big.
touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/motion_magnify.md

Motion magnification makes the movements in a video larger than they were: grass swaying in a breeze sways wide, a nod becomes a bow, a dancer's bounce leaps. What stands still stays where it is, so only what moves changes.

This effect measures how the picture moves and moves it further. It follows each part of the picture as it goes, keeps count of how far that part is from where it rests, and draws it that far again, several times over. It keeps the movements that go back and forth about as often as Timing says, so a sway can grow while a steady walk across the picture stays as it was. Where moving the picture further would fold it over itself, the movement is held back, so things stretch rather than tear. On the beats of the music the movement can grow for a moment, and it can follow the music's tempo.

USE
Drag motion_magnify.tox into your network. It shows TouchDesigner's sample movie of grass swaying in front of a painted wall, its sway made larger. Wire any TOP into input 1, and the result is on output 1 at the input's size. A camera on a tripod works best: when the camera moves, everything moves, and all of it grows. You can also wire audio or beat channels into input 2 and a depth map or mask into input 3 (below). Open Window shows the picture full size, and Esc closes the window.

THE PAGE
Amount says how much further things move: at 3 a movement is drawn 4 times as large. Timing says which movements grow, by how often they go back and forth: 0.5 a second for a slow sway or a breath, 1 for a nod, 2 or 3 for a bounce. Follow Tempo sets Timing from the music on input 2, one back and forth every two beats. Smoothness says how large the areas that move together are: lower lets small details move on their own, higher moves whole areas as one. Depth Focus, Depth Type and Max Distance are for input 3, and Beat Kick, Loudness and Song Moments are the music's.

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input. Run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. Each beat makes the movement grow for a moment (Beat Kick). Louder music makes it larger and quiet passages smaller (Loudness), eased over half a second. Follow Tempo keeps the movements that go back and forth with the music. With Song Moments on, a build makes the movement grow as it rises, and a drop makes it twice as large for Length seconds (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo. That clock is experimental and can still add or miss beats. TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. With nothing wired, the movement grows by Amount alone.

DEPTH OR MASK (input 3, optional)
With a depth map on input 3, Depth Focus keeps the larger movement to near things, so a person in front grows while the trees behind them stay as they were. With a mask (white inside), what lies outside it stays as it is. Depth Type reads depth cameras too. The RealSense and Kinect TOPs give distance from 0 to 1 (black where the camera got no reading, which is left out), and the Kinect Azure TOP gives meters, scaled by Max Distance. With nothing on input 3 everything grows.

FLASHING
Motion Magnify doesn't flash: it adds no light of its own and doesn't change how often anything goes back and forth, only how far, so it has no Flashing setting. Moving things further can still make a picture's own changes count where they didn't: a red edge swept across more of the picture crossed WCAG's saturated-red line more often (red flowers swaying), so every red shown is kept short of that line. Measured under a beat far busier than music, no test clip came near WCAG's three flashes a second, and at the defaults none added more than a flash a second to the clip's own (docs page, Accessibility).

PERFORMANCE
Measuring the picture's motion (optical flow) costs most of the frame. Everything about the movement is worked out at a quarter of the picture's size, and only moving the picture runs at its full size: about 3.6 ms on the GPU a frame at 1280 x 720 on an M2 Pro (docs page, Performance). At Amount 0 nothing runs but the picture passing through.

INSIDE
lum8, lum4, change, clock, lk8 and lk4 (lib optical_flow) measure the picture's motion, and pace and motion (lib flow_speed) turn it into a speed, held between the input's frames. move keeps the displacement: how far each part of the picture is from where it rests, carried along with the part as it moves, leaking back to 0 so that the rest follows what moves for long, and eased so that a shake or the flow's noise doesn't count (move_fb is its last frame). spread_h and spread_v spread it over its neighbors, so areas move together. stretch makes it larger (Amount, the music, Depth Focus) and works out how much that would stretch the picture, and guard holds it back wherever the picture would fold over itself. moved passes it on while Amount is above 0. warp takes each part of the picture from its displacement. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in): counts and steps turn its counts into steps, beat_now picks the beats, kick_env shapes each beat's push, beat_rate counts the beats a second for Follow Tempo, drop_env times a drop and level_smooth eases the loudness. Input 3 goes through depth and near (lib depth_near).

CREDITS
- Lagrangian motion magnification: the motion measured, then the picture moved further along it, after C. Liu, A. Torralba, W. T. Freeman, F. Durand and E. H. Adelson, "Motion Magnification" (SIGGRAPH 2005), who track points, group them into layers and move a layer further, offline. Followed, not copied. Later Lagrangian methods measure dense optical flow from a reference frame and warp the picture by it, such as P. Flotho, C. Heiss, G. Steidl and D. J. Strauss, "Lagrangian motion magnification with double sparse optical flow decomposition" (Frontiers in Applied Mathematics and Statistics, 2023); this effect wasn't built after them.
- The displacement added up frame by frame, carried with the content and leaking back to rest, so the effect runs on live video of any length with no reference frame, and the leak and its easing choose the movements' timing: original (no earlier source found). The carrying is semi-Lagrangian advection, as in J. Stam, "Stable Fluids" (SIGGRAPH 1999), and Pencil's strokes.
- The fold guard: a warp stays one to one, folding nothing over, while its displacement changes by less than a pixel per pixel, the kind of condition Y. Choi and S. Lee give for warping images ("Injectivity conditions of 2D and 3D uniform cubic B-spline functions", Graphical Models, 2000). Holding the displacement back locally, by the largest stretch nearby, is original (no earlier source found).
- Not used: Eulerian video magnification, which filters each pixel over time and adds the change back (H.-Y. Wu, M. Rubinstein, E. Shih, J. Guttag, F. Durand and W. T. Freeman, SIGGRAPH 2012), and its phase-based forms (N. Wadhwa, M. Rubinstein, F. Durand and W. T. Freeman, SIGGRAPH 2013, and their Riesz pyramids, ICCP 2014). They are patented in the US by MIT until 2032 to 2035 (US 9,805,475, 9,811,901, 9,324,005 and 9,338,331 among them), so this effect doesn't filter the picture's pixels over time and has no color (pulse) mode.
- The motion is this library's Lucas-Kanade optical flow (B. D. Lucas and T. Kanade, "An Iterative Image Registration Technique with an Application to Stereo Vision", 1981), shared with Datamosh, Fluid, Pencil and VHS + CRT. The red guard (lib red_guard.glsl) is shared with Bent Cam, Slit-Scan and VHS + CRT.
- Depth Type: the RealSense, Kinect and Kinect Azure TOPs' depth, as their help pages describe it; the depth reading (lib depth_near) is shared with Fluid, Paint, Particles, Pencil and Point Cloud.
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input): a kick is a jump in the low band's loudness; the beat clock takes its tempo from the gaps between kicks, each voting for the beat lengths it is a whole multiple of: inter-onset-interval tempo induction, the idea behind S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
Everything else is written for this library: Follow Tempo, the pushes on the beat and the depth focus. The flash limits: WCAG 2.3.1, measured with this library's tools. MIT.

EXPERIMENTAL
Fosfora's drops and raw audio's beats are experimental (AUDIO).
