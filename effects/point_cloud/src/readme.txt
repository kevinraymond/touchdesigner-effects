Point Cloud {v}: the picture as a cloud of points in 3D. touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/point_cloud.md

Every point of the picture is placed at its depth and drawn in its color, up to {n:,} of them. From the start the cloud looks just like the picture; as the camera sways round it, the depth shows: people stand out from the wall behind them, a floor runs back into the room. The music moves it: loudness swells the points, each beat deepens the cloud, each bar swings the camera to a new angle; with Fosfora's signal a build scatters the points and a drop blows them apart.

USE
Drag point_cloud.tox into your network: it shows TouchDesigner's sample movie (a birch among dark leaves) as a cloud, the bright trunk standing out in front. Wire your picture into input 1 and, for real depth, a depth map lined up with it into input 3 (a depth camera, Depth Anything): with nothing on input 3, or nothing but black, the picture's brightness stands in for depth (and every point stays). The result is on output 1, at the input's size. Optionally wire audio or beat channels into input 2 (below). Open Window shows it full size; Esc closes it.

INPUT 3
Brighter Is Nearer (Depth Type) for depth estimators and masks; Distance 0-1 for the RealSense and Kinect TOPs; Distance in Meters (Max Distance) for the Kinect Azure TOP. A depth camera's black (no reading) is left out of the cloud; don't invert a camera's depth yourself. Points where the depth jumps (the edge of a person against the wall) go to whichever side they are closer to, so nothing streaks between them; small steps in 8-bit depth are smoothed so walls don't show terraces. Points From: Everything, The Subject (what stands in front of what is beside it: people, without the floor and walls; Stand Out sets how much nearer) or Nearer Than Far Limit. With nothing on input 3 these two read input 1's brightness as depth, and only what is brighter than half (or than Far Limit, when that is higher) counts.

THE PAGE
Look: Points (solid dots), Glow (soft dots added up, glowing where they crowd) or Surface (Gaussian splats that blend into one skin, from Botsch, Hornung, Zwicker and Kobbelt 2005). Points From, Stand Out, Far Limit, Depth Type, Max Distance (above). Depth: how deep the cloud is; 0 is flat. Detail: 180, 270 or 360 rows of points. Size: of a dot, in steps between points. Color: the picture's, by depth (orange near, blue far) or white. Brightness. Turn and Tilt: where the camera looks from, in degrees; Distance: how near it is (1: the picture fills the frame). Sway: how far the camera swings by itself; Sway Time: seconds per swing. Loudness, Beat Swell, Bar Moves, Song Moments (below).

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input: run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. Loudness grows and shrinks the dots; each beat deepens the cloud for a moment and pops the dots (Beat Swell); each bar swings the camera to a new angle (Bar Moves); with Song Moments on, a build scatters the points off their places as it rises and a drop blows the cloud apart, after which it settles back (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo (experimental: it can still add or miss beats) and a bar every 4 beats; TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. Nothing wired: the cloud just sways.

INSIDE
depth (input 3, or input 1's brightness) -> near (the depth at the grid's size, as nearness, by Depth Type; near_tiny and depth_stats: is there any? if not, near_pick takes input 1's brightness) -> pos (each point in 3D: smoothed, edges put to a side, jittered within its cell so rows of dots don't make moire). For Points From The Subject or Nearer Than: near_small -> find, and keep picks which points stay. pivot_in, pivot_avg and pivot: the kept points' middle, eased, which the cloud turns around (pivot_fb holds last frame's). geo draws a square at every point by instancing (one per pixel of pos); dot_mat's shaders place each from pos, turn the cloud, add the music's moves and color it from source. cam stays where the picture fills the frame. render draws Points and Glow; for Surface, splat_depth draws the splats' depth a little farther back, splat_sum adds up their colors and weights over it (splat_mat), splat_norm divides one by the other; look picks one. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in); counts and steps turn its counts into steps, beat_env is a beat's swell, drop_env a drop's burst, build_lag the build, level_lag the loudness, bar and bar_lag the camera's angle for each bar.

CREDITS
- Surface: Botsch, Hornung, Zwicker and Kobbelt, "High-Quality Surface Splatting on Today's GPUs" (Eurographics Symposium on Point-Based Graphics, 2005), after Zwicker, Pfister, van Baar and Gross, "Surface Splatting" (SIGGRAPH 2001): a depth pass pushed back, the splats' colors and weights added up over it, then divided out.
- Smoothing: a bilateral filter (Tomasi and Manduchi, "Bilateral Filtering for Gray and Color Images", ICCV 1998).
- Points jittered within their cells against moire: stochastic sampling (Cook, "Stochastic Sampling in Computer Graphics", ACM TOG 1986).
- Placing the points: a pinhole camera, reading an estimator's depth as disparity (1 / distance).
- Depth edges: the "flying pixels" a depth map has between near and far are commonly left out; here each goes to the side it is closer to.
- Dice and jitter: pcg2d and pcg3d (Jarzynski and Olano, "Hash Functions for GPU Rendering", JCGT 2020).
- Read during the survey, not used: the Palette's kinectPointcloud (Derivative), FollowTheDarkside's td-movie-to-particle (no license stated).
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input): a kick is a jump in the low band's loudness; the beat clock takes its tempo from the gaps between kicks, each voting for the beat lengths it is a whole multiple of: inter-onset-interval tempo induction, the idea behind S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
- Flash limits: WCAG 2.3.1 (W3C, Three Flashes or Below Threshold), measured with this library's tools.
Everything else is written for this library; the depth reading (lib depth_near and depth_find) is shared with Particles. MIT.

UPDATING
Dragging a .tox in embeds a copy. To update, delete this one and drag in the new point_cloud.tox from https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/releases/latest (or point the COMP's External .tox at a downloaded copy). The version and the build it was saved in are on the About page.
