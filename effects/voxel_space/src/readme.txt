Voxel Space {v}: the picture as the terrain of an early 1990s flight game, its depth the hills and its colors the land, flown over to the music.
touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/voxel_space.md

In 1992 the helicopter game Comanche showed hills and valleys that looked solid at a time when computers could hardly draw a few hundred triangles. Kyle Freeman at NovaLogic called the method Voxel Space. The world was two pictures, a height map saying how tall the ground is at each point and a color map saying what color it is. For each column of the screen, the program stepped out from the camera across the maps, and each step drew a strip of its color as tall as its ground looked from that far away. Only the part rising above what was already drawn in that column showed, so nearer hills hid the ones behind them.

This effect does the same with your picture. Its colors are the land and its depth (input 3) the hills, so a dancer becomes a mountain range in her own colors moving as she moves. With nothing on input 3 the picture's brightness, softened, is the height. The camera flies over it, the bass lifts the hills, each beat dips the camera and the tempo sets the pace.

USE
Drag voxel_space.tox into your network. It shows TouchDesigner's sample movie of mist over a lake as terrain, flown over. Wire any TOP into input 1, and the result is on output 1 at the input's size. Wire a depth map into input 3 for hills from the depth (below), and music into input 2 to fly to it. Open Window shows the picture full size, and Esc closes the window.

THE PAGE
Flight picks how the camera flies. Fly goes forward over the picture, repeated mirrored at its edges, weaving along the seam where it meets its mirror image, so what stands in its middle passes on both sides, banking into its turns and keeping clear of the hills ahead. Orbit circles the picture as an island in a flat sea, looking down at it. Hover stands just off its near edge and looks across it, swaying from side to side. Speed is how fast the camera goes, Height how tall the hills stand, Smooth how rounded they are, Light how much a low sun from the left shades them, and Fog how soon the distance fades into the sky. Position, Altitude, Yaw, Pitch, Roll and Zoom move and turn the camera from where the flight puts it, each eased over about a third of a second, and with Speed at 0 they place a still camera. Pitch and Roll move and slant the horizon, as Comanche's did, so upright things stay upright, and zooming in slows the flight so the land crosses the view at the same pace. Bass Lift is how much the bass lifts the hills and Beat Kick how far each beat dips the camera. Depth Type says what input 3 holds. Flashing says how often a beat may dip the camera.

DEPTH (input 3)
A depth map is brightest where the floor runs up to the camera, so as heights it would make one long ramp. The floor is about as near all along each row of the picture, though, so each row's heights are measured from its own far baseline. The floor and the far wall lie flat, and whatever stands in front of them stands up. The tallest row sets the terrain's scale, eased over a second and a half, so hills from a depth map and from brightness come out about as tall. The heights and the colors both ease toward the picture's over a quarter of a second, so the hills move with the land and a video's noise doesn't shake them. Brighter Is Nearer suits depth estimators such as Depth Anything and masks. Distance 0-1 suits the RealSense and Kinect TOPs, and Distance in Meters the Kinect Azure TOP, scaled by Max Distance. Where a depth camera got no reading, the ground lies flat.

AUDIO (input 2)
The bass (the sub bass and bass bands) is smoothed over a second and lifts the hills, more while it is above its usual level over the last 4 seconds. Each beat dips the camera over a quarter of a second and lets it rise again over half a second, as often as Flashing allows. The tempo, counted over the last 6 seconds, sets the pace, from 0.75 to 1.3 times Speed with 120 BPM as 1. Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives the bands and beats from the sound itself. Fosfora's signal gives them from its feature bus. Run `fosfora --signal --feat-bus`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis works too. Without Fosfora's feature bus, its bass stands in for the low bands.

FLASHING
A dip moves the whole view down and back up, which counts as flashing, so a beat dips the camera only through a gate that follows the Flashing parameter. Off never dips it. Calm (the default) dips it at most once a second, and Up to 3 per second is the WCAG 2.3.1 limit. A flight moves the whole view too, passing bright and dark ground through it, and the near ground is the picture enlarged, so the land eases over a quarter of a second, the view over a tenth, and the camera flies slowly, slower still zoomed in: faster flight streamed a busy picture past fast enough to flash. The land sweeps its reds back and forth, so every pixel goes through the red guard, which keeps reds just short of WCAG's saturated-red line. The limits were measured on the output (docs page, Accessibility).

PERFORMANCE
About {perf} ms on the GPU a frame at 1280 x 720 on an M2 Pro (docs page, Performance).

INSIDE
colors eases the picture's colors over time (colors_fb is its last frame), render draws the view from them, a compute shader with one workgroup for each column of the picture, and settle eases the view over a tenth of a second (settle_fb is its last frame). Each of its 512 invocations projects one step out from the camera, each step a little longer than the last, a running minimum over the steps gives the highest point drawn so far after each one (the y-buffer), and each pixel finds the first step that reaches it, taking a cliff's face in the color of its top. flight carries the camera from frame to frame and eases its settings (flight_fb is its last frame, flight_size its size), with the sky's color from mean. The terrain starts at near, input 3 (or the picture's brightness, depth_guess, softened by depth_soft) at half size as nearness. rows finds each row's far baseline and how far above it the row reaches, span the tallest row's reach, relief the heights from them, smooth rounds them, ease eases them over time (ease_fb is its last frame), and terrain lights them. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in): lows, lows_mean, lows_now and lows_usual are the low end, smoothed over a second and its usual level over 4 seconds; counts and steps notice a beat, flash_limit and flash let a dip through as often as Flashing allows, and dip_env shapes it; tempo counts the beats a second, pace_ask turns them into a pace and pace eases it. drive holds the lift and the dip.

CREDITS
- Voxel Space: Kyle G. Freeman and NovaLogic, Comanche: Maximum Overkill (1992; US patents 5,550,959 and 6,020,893, both expired), the terrain drawn a screen column at a time front to back over a height map and a color map with a y-buffer.
- Sebastian Macke's write-up and code, "Voxel Space" (https://github.com/s-macke/VoxelSpace, code MIT), followed for the method; none of its code is used, and none of Comanche's maps, which it excludes from its license.
- The running minimum is a scan (W. D. Hillis and G. L. Steele, "Data parallel algorithms", CACM 29(12), 1986).
- Input 3 read as nearness through the library's depth_near.glsl, shared with Words, Pixel Art and others.
- The red guard (lib red_guard.glsl) is shared with VHS + CRT, Bent Cam, Motion Magnify, Slit-Scan and Scan Processor.
- The seven bands: Fosfora's split (its AUDIO-FEATURES.md), measured from raw audio by the library's audio input.
Everything else is written for this library: each row's heights from its own far baseline, the terrain's scale from its tallest row, the land easing as one, a workgroup a column with a binary search per pixel, the colors taken between steps and cliff faces in their tops' colors, the flights, and the music's lift, dip and pace.
