Reaction-Diffusion {v}: the live picture drawn by a growing reaction-diffusion pattern whose stripes follow its edges.
touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/reaction_diffusion.md

Reaction-diffusion is one way nature might paint a zebra. Two chemicals spread out and react, one feeding on the other, and from a few specks they settle into stripes, spots or coral. Alan Turing proposed it in 1952, and the Gray-Scott model is the version most people play with.

This effect grows that pattern over the live picture and steers it. The chemicals spread faster along the picture's edges than across them, so the stripes run along its contours, around an eye or down a stem, like a fingerprint. Lines draws the picture by the width of its stripes, as an engraving does, broad where it is dark and thin where it is bright. Dots and Coral change the pattern itself with the picture's darkness. On the beats of the music a ripple runs through the drawing, as often as Flashing allows.

USE
Drag reaction_diffusion.tox into your network. It shows TouchDesigner's sample movie of a river running over rocks, drawn in lines. Wire any TOP into input 1, and the result is on output 1 at the input's size. You can also wire audio or beat channels into input 2 and a depth map or mask into input 3 (below). Open Window shows the picture full size, and Esc closes the window. The picture shows at once as an even tone, and the pattern grows in from scattered seeds and takes over within a few seconds. Reset clears it, and it grows back the same way.

THE PAGE
Pattern picks Lines, Dots or Coral. Lines draws stripes along the edges, broad in the dark and thin in the light. Dots draws holes in the dark, a maze in between and spots in the light. Coral grows a branching coral, dense in the dark and scattered in the light. Size is Fine, Medium or Coarse, each twice the last, since the pattern grows on a grid the picture's size, half or a quarter of it. Fine shows faces and small things best and costs four times Medium. Follow Edges says how much the stripes turn to run along the picture's edges (at 0 they wander every way), and Speed how fast the pattern grows and reshapes as the picture changes. Where the picture moves, the pattern takes a moment to follow, which is part of its look.

Ink Level is how much ink the picture's middle brightness takes. Before that the picture's brightness is stretched between its darkest and brightest, followed over a second, so a gray river reads as well as a dark stage. Contrast says how strongly the brightness sets the ink: at 0 the pattern is the same everywhere and only its direction follows the picture. Colors draws Ink on Paper, the ink Over the Picture itself, Light on Dark (light lines where the picture is bright, on black) or Orange and Blue. Grow On, Sensitivity, Depth Type and Max Distance say where the pattern grows (below). Beat Kick, Loudness and Song Moments are the music's, and Flashing says how often a ripple may come.

GROW ON
Everywhere (the default) grows the pattern over the whole picture. Brightness grows it on what is bright. Movement grows it on what moved in the last second, so it trails a dancer and breaks up behind them. Depth (Input 3) grows it on what stands in front of its surroundings in the depth map. Floors and walls drop out, someone cut off by the left or right edge drops out too, and feet merge with the floor. Mask (Input 3) grows it inside the mask, which is white inside (MediaPipe, the NVIDIA Background TOP). Elsewhere the pattern breaks into spots and fades, and new seeds start it wherever it may grow. Sensitivity lowers the bar in each: dimmer brightness, slower movement, farther depth or a grayer mask counts as well.

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input. Run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. On the beats, as often as Flashing allows, a ripple runs out across the picture, each from another place, and the stripes it passes slide out and back as if seen through a wave in glass (Beat Kick says how far). It moves the drawing and not the pattern, so the pattern keeps its order. Louder music makes the lines bolder and quiet passages finer (Loudness), eased over half a second. With Song Moments on, a drop sends a broad, deep ripple out from the middle, through the same gate (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo. That clock is experimental and can still add or miss beats. TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. With nothing wired, the drawing is still.

DEPTH OR MASK (input 3, optional)
Input 3 is for Grow On Depth and Mask (above). Depth Type reads depth cameras too. The RealSense and Kinect TOPs give distance from 0 to 1 (black where the camera got no reading, which is left out), and the Kinect Azure TOP gives meters, scaled by Max Distance. With nothing on input 3, the picture's brightness stands in for depth.

FLASHING
A ripple slides the stripes, which are about as wide as a flash test's cells, so the stripes it passes swing between light and dark, and it counts as a flash. So does a drop's. Beats and drops pass through one gate together, which follows the Flashing parameter. Off never ripples, Calm (the default) ripples at most once a second (every second or third beat at dance tempos), and Up to 3 per second is the WCAG 2.3.1 limit (every beat up to 180 BPM). The pattern itself doesn't flash. Once grown in it holds its shape where the picture holds still, the lines' weight follows the picture over a quarter of a second and the loudness over half a second, and it starts as an even tone that turns into the pattern rather than growing in on blank paper. The limits were measured on the output (docs page, Accessibility).

PERFORMANCE
The pattern's steps cost most of the frame, and their cost grows with the grid's area, so Fine costs four times Medium and Coarse a quarter. Speed sets how many steps run each frame, up to 48.

INSIDE
grid is the picture at the grid's size. tensor, tensor_h, tensor_v and flow (lib structure_tensor) find the direction of its edges at every cell and how clear it is. moved holds what moved lately (grid_fb is the grid last frame), grow_from picks what Grow On needs, so the rest doesn't cook, and exposure finds the picture's darkest and brightest, eased over a second. where says where the pattern may grow and how much ink the picture asks for. seed takes the pattern from state_fb (the pattern last frame), sets each cell's kill rate and drops seeds where the pattern may grow but hasn't; react runs the Gray-Scott model's steps (its Passes, each reading the last), diffusing faster along the edges, and state_fb feeds it back. look draws it at the picture's size, ripples it on the beats and colors it. still is the empty grid the pattern starts from, and Reset sends state_fb back to it. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in): counts and steps turn its counts into steps, ask takes beats and drops as one, flash_limit and flash let them through as often as Flashing allows, ring times each ripple and beat_n turns where it starts, drop_now picks the ripples that came on a drop and drop_ring times their broad ripple, and level_smooth eases the loudness. Input 3 goes through depth, near and find (lib depth_near and depth_find).

CREDITS
- Reaction-diffusion: A. M. Turing, "The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis" (Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 237, 1952).
- The Gray-Scott model: P. Gray and S. K. Scott, "Autocatalytic reactions in the isothermal, continuous stirred tank reactor" (Chemical Engineering Science 38, 1983); its patterns as J. E. Pearson mapped them, "Complex Patterns in a Simple System" (Science 261, 1993). The feed and kill rates of each pattern here were found by running the model over Pearson's region of the map.
- Diffusion steered along a direction, so stripes follow it: A. Witkin and M. Kass, "Reaction-Diffusion Textures" (SIGGRAPH 1991).
- The direction of the picture's edges: the smoothed structure tensor (lib structure_tensor, shared with Paint), after J. E. Kyprianidis, H. Kang and J. Doellner, "Image and Video Abstraction by Anisotropic Kuwahara Filtering" (Pacific Graphics 2009) and J. E. Kyprianidis and J. Doellner (NPAR 2011), with the derivative filter of B. Jaehne, H. Scharr and S. Koerkel (1999).
- Lines crisp at any size: a Catmull-Rom bicubic (Catmull and Rom, 1974), shared with Datamosh and Fluid (lib common.glsl). Seeds fall where Chris Wellons' lowbias32 integer hash says (hash-prospector, public domain).
- Depth Type: the RealSense, Kinect and Kinect Azure TOPs' depth, as their help pages describe it; the depth reading (lib depth_near and depth_find) is shared with Fluid, Particles and Point Cloud.
- Read during the survey, not used: aaronmylespereira's TD-Toxes reactionDiffusion (MIT, made from free tutorials), paid Gumroad versions and a free Drive .tox (none stated). Each diffuses the same in every direction.
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input): a kick is a jump in the low band's loudness; the beat clock takes its tempo from the gaps between kicks, each voting for the beat lengths it is a whole multiple of: inter-onset-interval tempo induction, the idea behind S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
- Flash limits: WCAG 2.3.1 (W3C, Three Flashes or Below Threshold), measured with this library's tools.
Everything else is written for this library: the steering's second differences along the edges and across them (a usual way to discretize anisotropic diffusion, no one source followed), the seeding, the stretch of the picture's brightness, the engraving's line widths and the beat's ripple. MIT.
