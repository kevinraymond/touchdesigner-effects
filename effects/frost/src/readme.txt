Frost {v}: ice grown over the live picture from its edges and the frame, thawing where something is near or moves.
touchdesigner-effects, MIT.
Docs: https://github.com/kevinraymond/touchdesigner-effects/blob/main/docs/effects/frost.md

Frost grows on a cold window the way snow crystals grow in the air. Water freezing gives off heat, and an arm of ice that reaches a little ahead of its neighbors sheds that heat into colder air, so it grows faster still and leaves them behind. That is why ice grows in arms that branch, in ferns on a window and in six-armed stars in a cloud. Physicists model it with a phase field, a smooth map from air to ice whose edge moves wherever it is cold enough, and with simple rules on a hexagonal grid. Glass that is cold but not cold enough for ice gathers dew instead, droplets that grow and merge.

This effect grows that frost over the live picture. Crystals start on the picture's edges and creep in from the frame, where the room behind is far or dark, and a fern's arms run along the edges they grow over. What stands in front of its surroundings in the depth map, or whatever moves, warms the glass: the frost thaws there and grows back behind it. The ice blurs the picture behind it and glows where a light is behind it.

USE
Drag frost.tox into your network. It shows TouchDesigner's sample movie of a river running over rocks, and frost grows over it within a few seconds. Wire any TOP into input 1, and the result is on output 1 at the input's size. You can also wire audio or beat channels into input 2 and a depth map or mask into input 3 (below). Open Window shows the picture full size, and Esc closes the window. Reset clears the glass, and the frost grows back. A new Style clears it too.

THE PAGE
Style picks Fern, Stars or Dew (below). Size is Fine, Medium or Coarse, each twice the last: the ice grows on a grid the picture's size, half or a quarter of it, so Fine draws the finest crystals and costs four times Medium. Growth says how fast the frost grows and thaws. Crystals says how many new crystals start each second on the picture's edges where it is cold (in Dew, how quickly new droplets bead clear glass), and From the Rims how much frost creeps in from the frame's edges. Follow Edges says how much a fern's arms turn to run along the picture's edges; at 0 they all point one way.

Freeze Evenly, Thaw by Movement, Thaw by Nearness, Input 3 Holds, Sensitivity, Depth Type and Max Distance say where it is cold and where it is warm (below). Ice says how much the frost hides the picture behind it, Glow how much the light behind shows through, and Tint is the frost's color, a cold white. Beat Crystals, Loudness, Bass Thaw and Song Moments are the music's.

STYLES
Fern grows the ice as R. Kobayashi's phase field of a growing crystal, with six-fold arms. Fronds grow in from the frame and branch, crystals start on the picture's edges and their arms follow them, and where it has all frozen over, the arms (which froze first) stay whiter than the ice that filled in between them, so the ferns still show.

Stars grows C. A. Reiter's snow crystals on a hexagonal lattice: crisp six-armed stars of fine needles with side branches, all on the one lattice, as crystals of one grain of ice would grow. Each new star starts only where the air is still moist, away from the stars already grown.

Dew beads the glass with breath figures: droplets that start on the clear glass, grow as it condenses water and merge when they touch, leaving clear glass between the big ones for new small ones. Droplets too small to see one by one make a milky haze, and each bigger one is a small lens that shows the picture behind it turned over, with a glint.

COLD AND WARM
The glass is coldest where the room behind it is far, or with nothing on input 3, where the picture is dark, so the room freezes first and bright or near things last. Freeze Evenly raises the cold everywhere, so at 1 all of it freezes alike. Whatever moves warms the glass at once and leaves it to cool slowly behind it (Thaw by Movement), and with a depth map or mask on input 3, what stands out in front or is inside the mask warms it too (Thaw by Nearness). Warm glass melts the frost and starts no new crystals, and as soon as the warmth has gone the frost grows back in from the ice around it. A faint warmth (haze drifting through a dark room) doesn't count.

AUDIO (input 2, optional)
Fosfora's signal is the best input. Run `fosfora --signal`, drag fosfora_signal.tox (in Fosfora's integrations/touchdesigner/, https://github.com/kevinraymond/fosfora) into your project and wire it into input 2. Each beat starts new crystals along the picture's cold edges (in Dew, new droplets on the clear glass), makes the frost grow in a spurt and makes its edges and sparkles glint (Beat Crystals). Louder music makes the frost grow faster and quiet passages slower (Loudness). The bass warms what stands out in front, so the frost backs away from it on the bass (Bass Thaw, with a depth map or mask on input 3). With Song Moments on, a drop starts crystals all along the picture's edges at once (Fosfora's drop detection is experimental). Raw audio (Audio File In, Audio Device In) gives beats from a kick detector and a beat clock that locks to the kicks' tempo. That clock is experimental and can still add or miss beats. TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis gives beats from its kick or onset channels. With nothing wired, the frost grows at its own pace.

DEPTH OR MASK (input 3, optional)
A depth map, brighter nearer, says where the room is far (cold) and what stands in front of its surroundings (warm). Floors and walls don't count as standing out, someone cut off by the left or right edge of the frame drops out, and feet merge with the floor they stand on. With Input 3 Holds on Mask, what is inside the mask (white inside: MediaPipe, the NVIDIA Background TOP) warms the glass instead. Sensitivity says how easily something counts as standing out, or how gray a mask counts as inside. Depth Type reads depth cameras too: the RealSense and Kinect TOPs give distance from 0 to 1 (black where the camera got no reading, which is left out), and the Kinect Azure TOP gives meters, scaled by Max Distance. With nothing on input 3, the picture's brightness stands in for depth, and nothing warms the glass but movement.

FLASHING
Frost doesn't flash, so it has no Flashing setting. Its crystals grow and thaw by edges that move a few pixels a frame, the glass warms and cools over a fraction of a second and more, and a beat's glint brightens only thin edges and a few sparkles. The flashes were measured on the output under a beat far busier than music, for each style and at the far corners of the settings, and none adds to what the clip itself does (docs page, Accessibility).

PERFORMANCE
Each style costs 2.5 to 3 ms a frame on the GPU at 1280 x 720 at Size Medium on an M2 Pro. Fern's and Stars' steps cost the most, and their cost grows with the grid's area, so Fine costs about four times Medium and Coarse a quarter. Growth sets how many steps run each frame, up to 32.

INSIDE
grid is the picture at the ice's grid size. tensor, tensor_h, tensor_v, tensor_fb, tensor_ease and flow (lib structure_tensor) find the direction of its edges at every cell, eased over half a second. Input 3 goes through depth, near (nearness at a quarter size) and find (what stands out, or the mask), lib depth_near and depth_find. lib optical_flow (lum8, seen, change, change_avg, clock_fb, clock) measures what moves, and warmth (lib warmth, with warmth_fb) blends what stands out and what moves into one field that warms quickly and cools slowly. pane (with pane_fb) says how cold the glass is, how warm, and where crystals may start; edges hands it the picture's edges except in Dew, which needs none. seed takes last frame's ice from ice_fb, starts new crystals and counts how long each cell has been frozen; fern runs Kobayashi's steps (its Passes, each reading the last) and stars Reiter's, two passes a step; ice picks the Style's, and only that one cooks. look draws Fern's and Stars' ice over the picture. In Dew, dew_grow, dew_merge and dew_level step the droplets (dew_fb reads them back), and dew_look draws them; show picks the Style's look. clear is the nothing every Feedback TOP starts from, and Reset sends ice_fb and dew_fb back to it. Input 2 goes through audio (lib audio_in): counts and steps turn its counts into steps, glint times a beat's glint, level_smooth eases the loudness and bass_smooth the bass.

CREDITS
- The Fern style: R. Kobayashi, "Modeling and numerical simulations of dendritic crystal growth" (Physica D 63, 1993): the phase field, the six-fold anisotropy, the latent heat and the noise that sets off side branches, with his constants. Steered as T. Kim and M. C. Lin steered it for artists, "Visual Simulation of Ice Crystal Growth" (SCA 2003): the arms' direction set over the picture.
- The Stars style: C. A. Reiter, "A local cellular model for snow crystal growth" (Chaos, Solitons and Fractals 23, 2005), his beta and gamma for needle-like arms.
- The Dew style: breath figures as D. Beysens and C. M. Knobler watched them form, "Growth of Breath Figures" (Physical Review Letters 57, 1986), and as D. Fritter, C. M. Knobler and D. Beysens simulated them (Physical Review A 43, 1991): droplets that grow, merge into one at their center of volume when they touch, and leave clear glass for new ones.
- The direction of the picture's edges: the smoothed structure tensor (lib structure_tensor, shared with Paint, Reaction-Diffusion and Pencil), after J. E. Kyprianidis, H. Kang and J. Doellner, "Image and Video Abstraction by Anisotropic Kuwahara Filtering" (Pacific Graphics 2009), with the derivative filter of B. Jaehne, H. Scharr and S. Koerkel (1999).
- What moves: the library's optical flow input (lib optical_flow: its luma and its new-frame clock); the depth reading (lib depth_near and depth_find) is shared with Fluid, Particles, Point Cloud and Reaction-Diffusion. Crystals and droplets start where Chris Wellons' lowbias32 integer hash says (hash-prospector, public domain).
- Beats from raw audio (the library's audio input): a kick is a jump in the low band's loudness; the beat clock takes its tempo from the gaps between kicks, inter-onset-interval tempo induction, the idea behind S. Dixon, "Automatic Extraction of Tempo and Beat from Expressive Performances" (J. New Music Research, 2001). Followed, not copied.
- Flash limits: WCAG 2.3.1 (W3C, Three Flashes or Below Threshold), measured with this library's tools.
- Not used: frost or dew cleared by touch, Clipo's US 10,216,945 (a finger's trace clears a concealing image, redrawn after a set time) and Samsung's US 11,150,775 (a frost or vapor layer cleared by touch). The frost here takes no touch or pointer input, and cleared glass refreezes as the ice grows back.
Everything else is written for this library: the picture as the cold and the warmth, the glass drawing the heat away toward a temperature the picture sets, warmth melting the ice, the droplets kept a level a size in one atlas, the frost's look, and the beat's crystals, spurts and glints. MIT.
